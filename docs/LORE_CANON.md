# ENDO NINE — CANONICAL LORE & PROJECT CONTEXT

## Narrative Canon and Worldbuilding Source of Truth

This document establishes the official narrative canon of **Endo Nine**, the sequel to **Errant Night**.

This is development documentation and the project's narrative source of truth. All future development tasks involving environments, characters, enemies, stages, worldbuilding, visual design, dialogue, or narrative progression MUST consult and adhere to this document.

---

# PART I — ERRANT NIGHT: THE PREQUEL

## 1. The First Expedition

Humanity discovers a mysterious alien planet exhibiting unusual environmental and biological phenomena.

A three-member expedition is dispatched to investigate.

The expedition encounters an alien ecosystem and an ancient artifact of unknown origin.

Unbeknownst to humanity, the planet has already attracted the attention of an ancient technologically advanced civilization known as the **Overseers**.

The Overseers are guardians of balance. They possess extraordinary technology but are not primarily a military civilization.

They observe the planet's alien species and become increasingly alarmed by their biological capabilities.

The aliens reproduce, adapt, and evolve at extraordinary rates.

The Overseers interpret this rapid expansion as evidence of a civilization preparing to become an overwhelming interplanetary threat.

They decide to contain the planet.

## 2. The Freezing of the Planet

The Overseers deploy nine powerful artifacts across nine planetary regions.

Together, these artifacts establish a planetary freezing system capable of suppressing the alien ecosystem.

The planet is placed into an artificial frozen state.

During the first human expedition's encounter with the alien lifeforms, the situation deteriorates.

An alien parasite possesses one expedition member.

Another expedition member is critically injured, while the third dies.

The Overseers' freezing intervention also traps the surviving expedition members in stasis.

The Overseers subsequently withdraw from direct observation.

The planet becomes a frozen world containing a civilization whose development has been forcibly halted.

## 3. The Rescue Mission

When the first expedition fails to return, humanity dispatches a rescue crew.

The rescuers discover:

- Two surviving expedition members preserved in stasis.
- The remains of the deceased expedition member.
- A mysterious alien artifact.

They recover the expedition members and bring the artifact aboard their spacecraft.

They do not understand the biological danger associated with their discovery.

The alien parasite is brought aboard with them.

## 4. The Parasite and the Virus

The parasite is capable of possessing human hosts.

It also spreads a biological virus through the ship's ventilation system.

This creates multiple forms of infection and interference among the crew.

Some infected individuals experience profound biological changes.

Others become susceptible to manipulation or develop unusual abilities.

These events form the foundation of Errant Night's social-deduction gameplay.

The conflict is not simply between humans and an alien invader.

It involves infection, deception, possession, biological transformation, and competing survival objectives.

## 5. The Birth of the Alien

The parasite cannot simply transform its first host into the final Alien.

Creating the Alien requires a biological transfer involving the parasite's genetic material and genetic material obtained from its first human host.

These components are transferred into a separate human host.

The process consumes the parasite's life force and initiates a transformation lasting approximately 24 hours.

The resulting organism is the Alien: a new biological entity possessing extraordinary evolutionary potential.

The Alien is distinct from the original parasite.

## 6. The Seeker

The deceased member of the original expedition does not disappear entirely.

Their spirit remains connected to the events surrounding the planet and its artifact.

This spirit later possesses a member of the rescue crew, becoming the Seeker.

The Seeker's unusual perception reflects their supernatural connection to the expedition's tragedy.

## 7. The Canonical Ending of Errant Night

Although Errant Night supports multiple gameplay outcomes, the canonical ending leading into Endo Nine is:

**THE ALIEN WINS.**

The Alien survives the conflict and gains control of the spacecraft.

The surviving humans are unable to prevent its victory.

Rather than immediately traveling toward Earth, the Alien returns the ship to the frozen planet.

This begins Endo Nine.

# PART II — ENDO NINE: THE SEQUEL

## 8. The Return to the Frozen Planet

The Alien pilots the spacecraft back toward the frozen world.

The ship crashes near the location of the artifact recovered during Errant Night.

The crash threatens the remaining human survivors.

An Overseer intervenes and protects the crew from the worst of the impact.

The intervention leaves the Overseer severely injured and weakened.

The surviving crew eventually awakens on the planet.

They find themselves stranded on a hostile world whose frozen environment is beginning to change.

The Alien has survived.

## 9. The Overseer

The Overseer is a member of the ancient civilization responsible for freezing the planet.

Overseers are technologically sophisticated guardians who believe they are preserving cosmic balance.

Their intervention was motivated by fear of the aliens' extraordinary reproductive and evolutionary capabilities.

They believed that, if left unchecked, the alien civilization could eventually become an unstoppable threat.

However, this conclusion was based on an incomplete understanding of the species.

The injured Overseer initially remains convinced that the aliens must be contained.

Because of the crash and injuries, the Overseer cannot simply restore the planetary freezing system independently.

The Overseer must cooperate with the surviving humans.

This alliance becomes central to the sequel.

## 10. The Nine Artifacts

The planet contains nine major regions associated with nine Overseer artifacts.

The artifacts collectively maintain the planetary freezing system.

Only one artifact was encountered during the events of Errant Night.

The remaining artifacts are distributed throughout the planet.

Each artifact is connected to the environmental state of its surrounding region.

The artifacts are not ordinary monuments.

They are advanced technological devices capable of altering the planetary environment.

## 11. The Alien's Plan

The Alien discovers that the Overseer artifact can be reverse-engineered.

Using the knowledge and capabilities of a possessed human engineer, the Alien begins modifying the artifact's function.

Instead of preserving the planet's frozen state, the altered technology generates heat.

The process begins thawing the planet.

As temperatures rise, the alien ecosystem gradually reawakens.

Dormant organisms become active.

Alien biological systems resume reproduction, adaptation, and evolution.

The Alien intends to reverse the Overseers' containment of its species.

However, the Alien's actions also create a growing conflict with the surviving humans and the injured Overseer.

## 12. The Main Campaign — Eight Regions

The humans and Overseer embark on a journey through eight major regions of the planet.

Their immediate mission is to prevent the planetary thaw from becoming irreversible.

They must locate and restore the Overseer artifacts.

To accomplish this, they collect biological materials from the alien organisms inhabiting each region.

The Overseer uses these materials to modify the artifacts.

The goal is not merely to reactivate the original freezing system.

The modified artifacts must also account for the aliens' regenerative and evolutionary capabilities, preventing them from simply adapting to the containment mechanism.

Each region represents a different part of the planet's ecosystem.

As the crew advances, they encounter increasingly dangerous biological forms and environmental conditions.

The central conflict remains the same:

**Can the humans and Overseer stop the Alien before the planetary containment system is permanently compromised?**

# PART III — THE DIMENSIONAL LOOP

## 13. The Stage Eight Decision

At the conclusion of the eighth main-campaign region, the crew reaches a critical decision.

The injured Overseer attempts to transport the group toward the final confrontation.

The outcome depends on the decision made.

The correct decision allows progression toward the ninth stage and the final encounter with the original Alien.

The incorrect decision causes the transportation process to malfunction.

Instead of arriving at their intended destination, the crew is displaced into a dimensional loop connected to the planet's past.

This creates the alternate campaign route.

## 14. The Loop Timeline

The loop takes place several years before the events of the main campaign.

The planet has not yet reached the frozen condition encountered by humanity.

The crew experiences the alien civilization during an earlier period of its development.

The loop consists of eight regions or stages.

These regions demonstrate different levels of biological and civilizational development.

Importantly, the stages do not necessarily represent eight separate historical years.

They represent a progression through regions that exhibit increasingly sophisticated forms of alien life and civilization.

## 15. The Evolution of the Alien Civilization

The first loop region represents a relatively undeveloped part of the alien world.

The ecosystem is alive but comparatively primitive.

As the crew advances through subsequent regions, they encounter increasingly sophisticated alien biological systems.

Alien organisms reproduce rapidly.

They develop specialized structures, adapt to their surroundings, and transform their environments.

Their civilization becomes increasingly complex.

By the eighth loop stage, the crew witnesses the most advanced region of the civilization encountered during the loop.

This development demonstrates the species' extraordinary evolutionary potential.

However, the true significance of the loop is not simply how quickly the aliens develop.

It is **why they develop this way**.

## 16. The Overseer's Misinterpretation

The Overseers originally observed the alien civilization's explosive biological expansion.

They witnessed organisms multiplying rapidly and developing increasingly sophisticated capabilities.

They interpreted this growth as preparation for war.

To them, the aliens appeared to be building the biological equivalent of an overwhelming military force.

This assumption became the justification for freezing the entire planet.

The loop reveals a different truth.

The aliens were not necessarily preparing an invasion.

Their rapid reproduction and adaptation were natural characteristics of their species.

Their biological development was not inherently aggressive.

They were cultivating their world, expanding their ecosystems, and developing their civilization according to their own biology.

What the Overseers interpreted as military preparation was, in reality, a fundamental expression of alien life.

**Rapid growth does not automatically mean hostile intent.**

## 17. The Purpose of the Loop

The dimensional loop is not merely an additional series of combat stages.

It serves as the narrative revelation that challenges the Overseer's understanding of the planet.

The crew witnesses evidence that the aliens were capable of extraordinary development without necessarily seeking conquest.

The Overseer must confront the possibility that the original planetary freezing was based on fear and a mistaken interpretation.

The loop exposes the moral consequences of the Overseers' intervention.

An entire civilization was suppressed because its natural development appeared threatening to an outside observer.

This revelation becomes essential to the possibility of reconciliation.

# PART IV — THE FINAL CONFRONTATION

## 18. The Ninth Stage

The ninth stage is the final confrontation with the original Alien created during Errant Night.

This Alien is distinct from the ordinary alien creatures inhabiting the planet.

Its origin involves the parasite and human biological material.

The injured Overseer finally encounters the Alien directly.

The outcome of this confrontation determines the fate of the crew, the Overseer, and the alien civilization.

## 19. Bad Ending — The Alien's Victory

The Alien defeats the surviving humans and the Overseer.

The Overseer is killed.

The Alien obtains valuable intelligence from the defeated Overseer.

Armed with knowledge of the Overseers' civilization and technology, the Alien prepares to expand beyond the planet.

The Alien begins building a force capable of threatening the Overseers' homeworld.

The tragedy becomes self-fulfilling.

The Overseers originally feared that the aliens would develop into an expansionist threat.

Their intervention and the subsequent conflict ultimately contribute to producing that very outcome.

## 20. Normal Ending — The Planet Refrozen

The crew defeats the Alien.

The Overseer and surviving humans successfully restore the planetary containment system.

The planet is permanently refrozen, with additional safeguards against alien regeneration and adaptation.

The surviving humans escape.

Humanity survives, and the immediate threat ends.

However, the alien civilization remains suppressed.

The Overseers' original judgment is never fundamentally corrected.

This is a victory for human survival, but not necessarily a morally complete resolution.

## 21. Good Ending — Reconciliation

The crew experiences the dimensional loop and discovers the truth about the alien civilization.

They learn that the aliens' rapid reproduction and development were not inherently evidence of military aggression.

The Overseer is confronted with the consequences of the original containment decision.

During the final confrontation, the crew chooses a path that does not require exterminating or permanently freezing the alien civilization.

The original Alien is spared.

The Overseer and the aliens reach an understanding.

A treaty or agreement establishes the possibility of coexistence.

The planetary conflict ends through reconciliation rather than permanent suppression.

This ending represents the complete resolution of Endo Nine's central moral conflict.

# PART V — CENTRAL THEMES

## 22. The Meaning of Endo Nine

Endo Nine is a story about:

- Fear of the unknown.
- Biological evolution and adaptation.
- The consequences of misinterpreting another civilization.
- The difference between capability and intention.
- The moral cost of containment.
- Survival, cooperation, and reconciliation.

The Overseers were not necessarily malicious.

They acted on what they believed was a genuine threat.

The aliens were not inherently evil simply because they possessed extraordinary reproductive and evolutionary abilities.

The original Alien's actions are also not proof that every member of its species shares the same intentions.

The story deliberately distinguishes the actions of individuals from the nature of an entire civilization.

The dimensional loop is essential because it gives the characters evidence that their assumptions may have been wrong.

The good ending becomes possible through understanding, not merely superior combat strength.

# PART VI — CURRENT DEVELOPMENT CONTEXT

## 23. Current Prototype

**The existing playable prototype represents MAIN CAMPAIGN STAGE 1 (The Crash-Site Crater).**

- The game begins at the actual start of **Endo Nine**: after the Alien pilots the ship back to the frozen planet and crashes near the first Overseer artifact.
- Every run begins at the **crash-site crater** alongside the large wrecked spacecraft.
- The environment reflects a **freshly thawing alien world**—blending thawed dark soil, frost and snow patches, exposed crater rock, and reawakening alien biological flora.
- The **Dimensional Loop** (Part III) remains canonical as the later alternate campaign route triggered at Stage 8 that reveals the truth about the alien civilization.

## 24. Future Development Rules

Whenever implementing new content, consult this lore before making design decisions.

### Environments

Each region must reflect its established timeline, environmental condition, and place within the civilization's development.

Do not randomly introduce advanced alien structures into regions intended to represent earlier developmental stages.

### Alien Architecture

Alien structures may be biological, grown, organic, or integrated into living ecosystems.

Do not automatically portray alien civilization as conventional human-style industrial architecture.

### Enemies

Alien creatures should reflect the biological diversity and evolutionary characteristics of their species.

Hostile gameplay behavior does not automatically establish that the entire civilization is inherently malicious.

### The Overseer

The Overseer must remain consistent with their established identity as an ancient technological guardian whose original judgment is challenged by the loop.

### Story Progression

Preserve the distinction between the main campaign and the dimensional loop.

Do not merge their timelines or treat Loop Stage 1 as the game's opening crash sequence.

### Endings

Preserve all three established outcomes:

- Bad: Alien victory and expansionist retaliation.
- Normal: Alien defeated and planet permanently refrozen.
- Good: Reconciliation following the loop's revelation.

Do not invent a replacement ending without explicit authorization.
